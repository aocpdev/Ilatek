Roof pressure washing: preparation, not cosmetics

A sealant does not bond to dirt: it bonds to the surface. That is why pressure washing is the step that decides whether a coating lasts or peels off in the first summer.

Short answer

Roof pressure washing costs from $250. It includes washing with pressure controlled per surface, removal of dirt and mold, drain cleaning after washing, a check of the roof condition and preparation for sealing. It is recommended once a year, or twice with heavy mold, nearby trees or salt breeze.

What roof pressure washing includes

  • Washing with pressure controlled per surface.
  • Removal of dirt, mold and residue.
  • Drain cleaning after washing.
  • A check of the roof condition.
  • Preparation for sealing or waterproofing.

Pressure is adjusted to the material: a concrete slab and a metal or tile roof do not take the same treatment, and the goal is a clean surface, not a lifted one.

Why wash before sealing

Dirt, mold and salt form a layer between the roof and the sealant. If that layer is there, the product bonds to it and not to the roof: it lasts less, lifts in sheets and the leak returns the way it was going to enter. Mold also holds moisture, and applying over trapped moisture is the classic mistake of systems that do not breathe.

So the right order for a roof that will be coated is: clear the drainage (roof and gutter cleaning), wash the surface, then apply the system. Skipping the wash saves one invoice and charges the difference in the warranty.

When it is worth it

  • Before sealing: always. Adhesion depends on a clean surface.
  • When the roof looks stained or has fungus: washing restores the color and stops the buildup.
  • Once a year as maintenance: or twice with heavy mold, nearby trees or salt breeze.

A typical residential roof is washed in a few hours; large roofs can take the full day, depending on area and soiling. The condition check done during washing is what tells you whether the roof needs sealing, repair or ponding correction correction.
