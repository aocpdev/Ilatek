(14) Cookies and Similar Technologies

This section explains what cookies and other similar technologies are, what categories may be used on our site, what information may be collected through them and how you can control them. It is part of the disclosure of our privacy practices in accordance with Law No. 39-2012 (Puerto Rico Privacy Policy Notification Act), Regulation No. 8568, Law No. 172-2022, the provisions of the Federal Trade Commission (FTC) on deceptive practices and, where applicable, the Children's Online Privacy Protection Act (COPPA).

1. What are cookies and similar technologies

Cookies are small text files that a website saves on your browser or device when you visit it. Similar technologies, such as tracking pixels or tags, browser local storage, scripts, and web beacons, may be used in conjunction with them to perform similar functions.

These technologies allow the site to remember information from page to page or between visits, recognize the browser used and record certain interactions. Some are set by the site itself and others may be set by third-party services that the site uses.

2. Categories of cookies and similar technologies

CategoryWhat it does and what it is used for
Strictly necessary and functionalThey allow the site to load, display correctly, and operate securely. They include, for example, session maintenance, securing forms, loading content, and registering basic preferences. Without them, some parts of the site cannot function.
Performance and analyticsThey collect statistical information about how the site is used: pages visited, time spent, traffic origin and technical errors. They are used to measure performance and improve the content and browsing experience.
Advertising and marketingThey allow us to measure the effectiveness of our campaigns, attribute a request or conversion to the campaign that originated it and, when enabled, show ads to people who have already interacted with the site (remarketing or retargeting).
Third party cookies and similar technologiesThey refer to the origin of the technology rather than its purpose: they are established by third-party providers whose services we use, such as the site platform, analytics tools, advertising pixels or embedded widgets. They can fulfill necessary, measurement or marketing functions.

3. Information that may be collected

Depending on the technology used and the site configuration, these tools may collect or record, where applicable:

  • IP address and approximate technical location data derived from it.
  • Browser and device information: browser type and version, language, screen resolution, device type, and user agent.
  • Operating system.
  • Pages visited, navigation paths, links used and activity within the site.
  • Date, time and duration of the visit.
  • Interaction with site content, such as scrolling, clicking, playing videos, or using forms and the quoter.
  • Pseudonymous identifiers generated by analytics, advertising, and attribution tools, including cookie or device identifiers.

This information may be statistical and not directly identify you, or it may be associated with data that you voluntarily provide to us on a form, in which case it is treated in accordance with sections 2, 3 and 10 of this Policy.

4. What are these technologies used for?

  • Maintain site functionality and security, including protecting forms from automated submissions and abuse.

  • Understand visitor behavior to improve the structure, content and clarity of information.
  • Measure the effectiveness of our advertising campaigns.
  • Improve site functionality and user experience.
  • Support attribution marketing and, where enabled, remarketing advertising to people who have already visited the site.

Some browsers allow you to activate a “Do Not Track” (DNT) signal, and there are additional privacy preference signals, such as Global Privacy Control (GPC). These signals do not have a uniform technical interpretation and their treatment depends on each browser and each provider.

As of this update, ILATEK does not have its own mechanism implemented that automatically detects and responds to DNT or GPC signals, so we do not claim that the site recognizes or respects them. The most direct way to limit these technologies is the browser configuration described in point 7, together with the controls offered by the providers themselves.

  • If you delete cookies, you may need to reset your preferences on subsequent visits.
